 Summary

Most of the listed stocks have become more volatile since the start of 2026, and six of the eight reserves would move to a lower collateral factor. METAc and TSLAc would keep their current values. Maximum liquidation bonuses, liquidation fees, collateral risk, caps, and the USDC interest rate curve are unchanged.

 The new collateral factors would take effect as a new risk configuration on each of the six reserves. No position would be liquidated by the update. 
 Motivation 
 Stock volatility has risen since the start of 2026. The recent volatility of the typical S&P 500 stock, the geometric mean over the index members, has gone from 27.9% to 34.1% a year, and six of the seven stocks behind the Magnificent Seven (Mag7) tokens on the hub have followed, MSFT the most (19.4% to 33.5%) and AAPL the least (22.5% to 25.0%). TSLA, the most volatile of the seven, eased slightly, from 50.6% to 49.2%. Recent volatility weights each daily return more the more recent it is, with half the weight on the last 46 sessions.

 The collateral factor of each reserve is derived from its stock’s recent volatility, and the proposed values are set on the latest data. 
 Collateral Factors 
 In Aave V4 the collateral factor is both the borrowing limit and the liquidation threshold. For a stock priced 24/5, it has to cover the largest fall from any off-hours price to the lowest price before five minutes past the next regular open, plus a 0.5% allowance for the feed’s deviation threshold. The haircut, the fall the collateral factor is built to absorb, is sized so that this fall exceeds it on one night per stock in ten years, with a 2020-type crash counted as one year in ten. 
 The haircut scales a forecast of the stock’s daily volatility over the next year by the size of a once-in-ten-years off-hours fall relative to daily volatility, measured across S&P 500 stocks. The forecast weights the stock’s recent moves and pulls them partly toward the market-wide level. The collateral factor is what remains after the haircut, the maximum liquidation bonus, and an allowance for interest while a position waits for liquidation, rounded down to a whole point and capped at 65%.

 Implementation 
 The proposed collateral factors would be added as a new dynamic risk configuration on each of the six reserves, with their current maximum liquidation bonus and liquidation fee. In Aave V4 new positions open under the latest configuration, and an existing position moves to it when it borrows, withdraws, or changes the assets it uses as collateral; such an action goes through only if the position is healthy under the new collateral factors. No position would be liquidated by the update. 
 Specification 
 The update applies to the Mag-7 Spoke of the Equities Hub on Base. Collateral risk, caps, the dynamic liquidation bonus configuration, and the USDC interest rate curve are unchanged.

## post 2 — o_expxresearch — 2026-11-23
 Support, from a USDC lender in the Equities Hub market (yoUSD Edge) 
 We support this ARFC. Lowering the CFs on MSFTc, AAPLc, GOOGLc, AMZNc, NVDAc and SPCXc to reflect the latest volatility is the right use of the methodology, and it is good for lenders. 
 Why it helps lenders. We re-ran our weekend- and worst-day gap tests on the proposed CFs. At 65% (5.5% bonus), a stock can fall about 31.4% in one jump before a loan at its limit stops covering debt plus bonus, against 16.7% to 26.2% at today’s limits for MSFTc, AAPLc, GOOGLc, AMZNc and NVDAc. Updating the CFs when volatility has shifted (the typical S&P 500 stock went from 27.9% to 34.1% recent volatility) is what keeps lending against these tokens safe. 
 The same logic applies to ANTc in the companion ARFC. The reasoning here is that the CF has to follow the current volatility regime. By that standard, ANTc is the least supported listing in the batch: 
 - It has one session of its own return history, so its 45% CF rests on stand-ins (a pre-IPO perp and past IPOs) and not on the recent-volatility estimate this ARFC uses for every other reserve. 
 - Its first-night fall already exceeded the 99th percentile of the IPO sample the proposal cites. 
 - The early lock-up releases (up to 303M shares on 2026-12-15, then 303M, 353M and 353M through 2027-02-02) are about 134% of the 980M-share IPO float. This is a supply event the stand-ins don’t reflect. 
 - The CF review is set for the last session of December, with decreases phased in over 10 sessions, so a cut after the Dec 15 release would not bite until January. 
 So we would ask LlamaRisk and the DAO to do one of two things: 
 1. Leave ANTc out of the current extension and list it after the Dec 15 release and its first earnings, when its own returns can feed the method. 
 2. If ANTc lists in this batch, revisit its CF (and cap) before Dec 15, not at the year-end review, and avoid cap increases until the Jan 5 release has passed.
